Since ChatGPT gained national prominence in November 2022, the ethical facets of AI use have become more and more complicated. Amaranth helps humanists to navigate the fast-changing dynamics of the debates over ethical uses of generative AI products.
{: .lead}

## The University of New Mexico's Statement of Values

The AI Steering Committee at UNM has drafted a [Statement of Values,Ethics, and Principles for
Artificial Intelligence at UNM](https://ai.unm.edu/guidelines-policies). This document provides an excellent framework for an ethical approach to use of generative AI tools for teaching and research. At Amaranth, we offer advice on how to match the goals of documents like this one to specific situations in the classroom and in the archive. 

## Local LLM

With a local LLM, you can keep all of your interactions with generative AI on your own device, never interacting with corporate data centers after the initial download. Although the best quality local LLMs were created using the ethically questionable processes of the frontier models (see below), when using them you have complete [data sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_sovereignty) and do not contribute any data or money to anyone.

We are currently researching local LLM, the ability to run a large langauge model on your own laptop. With promising results in its early stages, we are at this time able to recommend the app [Ollama](https://ollama.com/) as the best choice for getting started with local LLMs. Please email us at <amaranth@unm.edu> if you are interested in joining our local LLM study group.

## Using Frontier AI Models

At this time, an advanced use of generative AI using local LLM requires much more than 32GB of RAM on a device, and that comes at a high price point. If you want to use AI to crunch big data or produce the most sophisticated analyses, then you will probably need to use a corporate frontier model such as Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's ChatGPT, or Google's Gemini. An ethical use of these applications requires understanding some background about how they were made.

All of the frontier AI models were created by a process of large-scale data scraping which likely violated intellectual property law and copyright law. [Litigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_v._Microsoft_and_OpenAI), with *The New York Times* as lead plaintiff, is currently pending. In addition, the models in their initial stages require human beings to censor anti-social output deemed unfit for public release. The frontier AI companies employed low-wage labor for this work, known as [Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement_learning_from_human_feedback). The construction of [data centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_center#Socioeconomic_and_environmental_impacts) to run the frontier AI models, in the past and in regard to future plans, has been criticized on environmental justice grounds. 

Keeping these ethical considerations in mind implies that some limitations should be placed on the use of frontier AI models. For some this means using frontier AI models sparingly, and only when local LLM is not feasible. For others this means using frontier AI models while searching for ways to choose and promote companies that address ethical considerations with something more than lip service.

## Consider What Use Fits Your Project

For both local LLMs and frontier AI models, an ethical use of AI will often hue closely to the purpose of the specific project. For a research paper submitted as a course requirement, the instructor will provide guidance on what parts are AI-friendly and what aspects of the research process must be done without AI. Ethical use, in this case, means following the guidelines from the course instructor.

Scholars considering the use of AI when preparing a piece for publication face a different set of ethical questions. The debates over AI disclosure continue and rules in place today might not apply a year from now. Princeton University Library maintains a helpful [guide on disclosure policies](https://libguides.princeton.edu/generativeAI/disclosure) from several large academic publishers.
